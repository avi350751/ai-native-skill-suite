# Maturity and Learning

Use this reference for an experienced person or team that has domain capability but uneven AI understanding.

## Baseline profile

Capture:

- role, function, seniority, span of control and decision rights;
- business outcomes and measures the person owns;
- critical workflows, stakeholders, systems and data classes;
- current AI usage, approved tools and failed experiments;
- technical fluency without equating it to AI maturity;
- appetite for change, available time and organizational incentives;
- regulatory, security, employment, customer and reputational constraints.

## Capability dimensions

Rate 0–4 and cite evidence for each:

1. **AI literacy:** capabilities, limits, model variability and fit-for-purpose selection.
2. **Domain-AI translation:** ability to convert tacit expertise into context, criteria, examples and exceptions.
3. **Workflow redesign:** ability to rethink the full process, not bolt AI onto one step.
4. **Evaluation:** representative tests, quality thresholds, monitoring and failure analysis.
5. **Data and tool readiness:** lawful access, quality, provenance, integration and maintainability.
6. **Risk and accountability:** ownership, human oversight, escalation and evidence.
7. **Adoption leadership:** stakeholder involvement, coaching, incentives and feedback.
8. **Value realization:** baseline, benefits, costs, trade-offs and sustained measurement.

Classify the person separately from the team. A capable individual inside an unready process needs a different plan from a novice manager with a technically strong team.

## Learning journey

### Domain + AI

Goal: use AI safely on bounded expert work.

Practice: test model strengths and failure modes on sanitized cases; build context packets; compare outputs against expert criteria; establish personal safe-use rules.

Proof: two or three reviewed artifacts with time, quality and rework evidence.

### Workflow designer

Goal: redesign a recurring process and orchestrate a checked loop.

Practice: map decisions and handoffs, select AI roles, build evaluation cases, define approval and escalation, test with actual users.

Proof: before/after workflow, pilot results, control design and operational guide.

### AI-enabled leader

Goal: create the conditions for a team to adopt, learn and improve responsibly.

Practice: prioritize a portfolio, coach different roles, align incentives, set metrics, govern exceptions and communicate trade-offs.

Proof: team capability plan, reviewed use-case portfolio, sustained outcome dashboard and lessons from a stopped or revised experiment.

## 30/60/90-day default

- **Days 1–30 — Understand and assist:** diagnostic, task inventory, safe experimentation, three H1 cases and a baseline.
- **Days 31–60 — Redesign and pilot:** map one material workflow, build an H2 pilot, test representative and edge cases, train affected users.
- **Days 61–90 — Operationalize and lead:** review evidence, standardize what works, close control gaps, define support and propose an H3 option without assuming it should be funded.

## Development plan format

For each capability include:

| Capability | Current evidence | Target behavior | Practice in real work | Proof | Support needed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Keep learning roughly aligned to current work. Courses may supply vocabulary; transformation capability comes from supervised application and reflection.

## Readiness signals

An AI-enabled mid-career leader can:

- reject a fashionable use case that lacks value or control;
- translate expert judgment into explicit criteria and examples;
- explain the workflow's failures and escalation path;
- run a bounded experiment without presenting it as production-ready;
- distinguish user adoption from measurable business benefit;
- involve security, legal, data, HR or employee representatives at the right point;
- stop, revise or scale based on evidence.
