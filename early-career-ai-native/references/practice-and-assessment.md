# Practice and Assessment

Assess what the learner can do reliably, not what they can describe or how polished an AI-produced artifact appears.

## Practice design

Use a progression of increasing independence:

1. **Observe:** critique a worked human-AI interaction and identify errors, missing context and unsafe assumptions.
2. **Complete:** finish a partially specified task using supplied sources and a verification checklist.
3. **Adapt:** apply the workflow to a new but similar situation and explain changes.
4. **Design:** build a workflow from a real task, including controls and success measures.
5. **Transfer:** teach or document the workflow so another person can reproduce it.

Include at least one failure exercise. Ask the learner to find a plausible but wrong answer, explain why it failed and improve the workflow.

## Portfolio capstone

A strong capstone contains:

- a real problem and intended beneficiary;
- the original workflow and baseline;
- constraints, approved inputs and risks;
- allocation of human and AI responsibilities;
- prompts, context or automation needed to reproduce the work;
- evaluation cases, including edge cases;
- results against quality, time, rework or learning measures;
- known limitations, escalation path and improvement backlog.

Do not reward complexity for its own sake. A well-controlled two-step loop can demonstrate more maturity than an opaque autonomous agent.

## Rubric

Score each dimension 1–4 and cite artifact evidence.

| Dimension | 1 — Emerging | 2 — Assisted | 3 — Independent | 4 — AI-native |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Problem framing | vague task | goal stated | users, constraints and criteria clear | reframes the work around value |
| Context | little or irrelevant | basic context | curated and traceable | reusable context system with provenance |
| Human/AI roles | AI does everything | review implied | decisions and handoffs explicit | complementary roles designed intentionally |
| Verification | trusts output | spot-checks | layered checks tied to risk | evaluation loop detects and learns from failure |
| Domain quality | superficial | mostly correct | sound judgment and exceptions | creates useful insight without overclaiming |
| Responsible use | risks missed | follows checklist | identifies and mitigates risks | builds safeguards and escalation into workflow |
| Reproducibility | outcome only | partial notes | another person can repeat it | workflow is maintainable and observable |
| Outcome evidence | anecdotal | output looks better | meaningful before/after measure | sustained improvement and trade-offs explained |

Readiness for independent use normally requires no dimension below 2, at least 3 in verification and responsible use for the target task, and evidence across more than one example.

## Review questions

- What did AI contribute that was genuinely useful?
- Where did human judgment change or reject the result?
- What evidence supports the final output?
- What failed, and what was changed in the loop?
- What data or action should never enter this workflow?
- Would the learner recognize when the method no longer fits?
- Can someone else reproduce the result from the documentation?

## Workplace proof options

If public portfolio work is inappropriate, accept a sanitized process map, synthetic example, supervisor-attested result, redacted evaluation summary or private demonstration. Never pressure the learner to expose employer, client, patient or research data.
