# Stream Map

Use this map to personalize without assuming that one degree or job title predicts the learner's needs. Classify the person by the work they perform, the artifacts they create, the decisions they support and the consequences of error. Combine streams when needed.

## Starting-pattern adaptations

| Starting pattern | Preserve | Build next | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Technical or computing | logic, systems thinking, testing | problem framing, user context, evaluation, governance | reducing AI-native work to coding |
| Quantitative or analytical | measurement, models, structured reasoning | unstructured context, communication, causal caution | treating generated claims as data |
| Business or commerce | value, process and stakeholder awareness | data literacy, experimentation, workflow instrumentation | vague productivity promises |
| Creative or communication | audience sense, narrative, iteration | provenance, evaluation, rights and brand constraints | confusing rapid generation with original judgment |
| Humanities or social science | interpretation, research, ethics, writing | structured workflows, data/tool fluency, reproducibility | undervaluing qualitative judgment |
| People, service or education | empathy, facilitation, situational judgment | documentation, privacy, rubric design, supervised automation | automating consequential human decisions |
| Frontline, field or trades | practical constraints, safety, tacit knowledge | accessible interfaces, capture of context, escalation design | desktop-only or text-heavy assumptions |
| Low digital access or confidence | lived/domain knowledge | basic file, search, data and security habits | tool overload or shame-based framing |
| Career switcher | transferable judgment and work habits | new domain vocabulary, proof artifacts, gap-specific practice | restarting from zero unnecessarily |

## Work streams

### Software, IT, data and engineering

- Entry tasks: explain code, draft tests, query data, document systems, troubleshoot from approved evidence.
- AI-native progression: specification-first work, agent-assisted implementation, automated evaluation, observability and secure human approval.
- Evidence: tested change, reproducible notebook, incident analysis or documented workflow.
- Watchouts: insecure code, fabricated APIs, unreviewed changes, secrets, dataset leakage and automation without rollback.

### Natural sciences, research and laboratory work

- Entry tasks: literature maps, protocol explanation, code assistance, data cleaning plans and hypothesis critique.
- AI-native progression: traceable research loops connecting sources, methods, analysis, review and experiment planning.
- Evidence: cited synthesis, reproducible analysis, protocol critique and uncertainty log.
- Watchouts: invented citations, unsupported causal claims, research integrity, sensitive data and irreproducible analysis.

### Business, management and entrepreneurship

- Entry tasks: market framing, meeting synthesis, customer-pattern analysis, spreadsheet explanation and business writing.
- AI-native progression: evidence-linked decision workflows, scenario loops and small operating automations.
- Evidence: decision memo, customer insight synthesis, unit-economics model with assumptions or tested process improvement.
- Watchouts: confidential strategy, fabricated market facts, weak assumptions and premature customer-facing automation.

### Finance, accounting and economics

- Entry tasks: explain statements, reconcile bounded datasets, draft variance commentary and explore scenarios.
- AI-native progression: controlled analysis pipelines, exception detection and decision-support loops with audit trails.
- Evidence: reconciled workbook, sourced analysis, documented checks and sensitivity analysis.
- Watchouts: investment advice, model risk, stale data, privacy, regulatory requirements and arithmetic hidden in prose.

### Law, policy, public administration and compliance

- Entry tasks: issue spotting, document comparison, plain-language summaries and structured research plans.
- AI-native progression: jurisdiction-aware research and review workflows with provenance and accountable sign-off.
- Evidence: source-linked memo, clause comparison, policy impact matrix or controlled intake workflow.
- Watchouts: unauthorized legal advice, outdated law, privilege, personal data, fabricated authorities and missing jurisdiction.

### Health, life sciences and social care

- Entry tasks: learning support, administrative drafts, literature synthesis and non-diagnostic communication.
- AI-native progression: governed support workflows with clinical or professional oversight and explicit escalation.
- Evidence: cited education artifact, de-identified process improvement or reviewed operational workflow.
- Watchouts: diagnosis/treatment, patient data, bias, consent, local clinical rules and automation of high-stakes decisions.

### Arts, design, media and communications

- Entry tasks: ideation, audience variants, critique, research organization and production planning.
- AI-native progression: iterative creative systems that preserve authorship, provenance, brand intent and human direction.
- Evidence: concept rationale, source/provenance record, tested content variants or documented creative workflow.
- Watchouts: copyright, likeness, attribution, brand safety, manipulation and homogeneous output.

### Humanities, languages and social sciences

- Entry tasks: compare interpretations, build reading maps, practice language, code qualitative data and edit prose.
- AI-native progression: source-grounded inquiry loops that distinguish evidence, interpretation and counterargument.
- Evidence: annotated synthesis, argument map, coding framework or reflective revision trail.
- Watchouts: fabricated quotations, flattening contested views, cultural bias and outsourced thinking.

### Education and learning services

- Entry tasks: differentiated explanations, practice questions, rubric drafts and feedback support.
- AI-native progression: adaptive learning loops governed by teacher judgment and evidence of actual learning.
- Evidence: lesson sequence, assessed learner improvement, rubric-tested material and accessibility adaptation.
- Watchouts: student privacy, cheating, inaccessible content, biased assessment and over-personalization without evidence.

### Operations, supply chain, hospitality, field work and skilled trades

- Entry tasks: SOP explanation, shift handover, checklist creation, inventory analysis and troubleshooting support.
- AI-native progression: context-aware operating loops connecting plans, observations, exceptions and escalation.
- Evidence: safer checklist, improved handover, exception workflow or measured reduction in rework.
- Watchouts: physical safety, stale instructions, poor connectivity, labor impacts and actions without supervisor approval.

### Sales, marketing and customer service

- Entry tasks: account research from approved sources, message drafts, call practice, tagging and response summaries.
- AI-native progression: coordinated customer workflows with consent, quality monitoring and clear human ownership.
- Evidence: reviewed outreach experiment, insight report, response-quality evaluation or escalation design.
- Watchouts: spam, deceptive personalization, unsupported claims, customer data and autonomous commitments.

### Human resources and people operations

- Entry tasks: policy explanation, learning content, structured interview practice and de-identified theme synthesis.
- AI-native progression: employee-service and talent workflows with transparency, fairness review and human accountability.
- Evidence: improved self-service content, reviewed learning path or process map.
- Watchouts: employment decisions, surveillance, protected attributes, bias and confidential personnel data.

### Interdisciplinary, undeclared or new fields

Do not force a label. Derive the path from five questions: What outcome is produced? What inputs are trusted? What judgment is scarce? What failure would matter? What artifact can demonstrate competence? Select methods from the closest streams and explain the combination.

## Cross-stream fundamentals

Every learner should be able to:

- frame a problem before choosing a tool;
- assemble a lawful, relevant context packet;
- request structured output and acceptance criteria;
- verify facts, calculations, sources and domain claims;
- disclose material AI assistance when required;
- distinguish draft, recommendation, decision and action;
- maintain a correction loop and evidence of improvement;
- explain when not to use AI.
