# Executive Diagnostic

## Executive learning outcomes

A capable AI-native executive does not need to be a model engineer. They should be able to:

- explain what current AI can and cannot reliably do in the enterprise's context;
- distinguish generation, retrieval, prediction, recommendation, coordination and action;
- interrogate evidence, evaluation, data provenance, full economics and vendor claims;
- decide which judgment and accountability must remain human;
- recognize when a workflow, control or business model must change;
- set risk appetite and escalation consistent with affected stakeholders;
- lead workforce and operating-model change rather than delegate it to IT;
- govern a portfolio through learning, scale and stop decisions.

## Rapid alignment session

For a leadership team, structure the discussion around five questions:

1. What has become materially possible for our customers, mission and operations?
2. What remains unreliable, costly, constrained or inappropriate?
3. Where do we possess distinctive context, data, trust, distribution or judgment?
4. Which decisions and consequences remain ours even when AI participates?
5. What will we learn in the next 90 days, and what evidence would change our strategy?

Use examples from the enterprise's value streams. Avoid tool demonstrations without decision relevance.

## Maturity dimensions

Rate each dimension 0–4 with evidence, variation by business unit and confidence level.

| Dimension | Diagnostic focus |
|---|---|
| Strategic clarity | value pools, stakeholder outcomes, explicit choices and non-goals |
| Leadership fluency | shared mental model, decision quality and visible sponsorship |
| Use-case portfolio | balance, prioritization, ownership and stopping discipline |
| Workflow/operating model | end-to-end redesign, decision rights and human-AI coordination |
| Data and context | access, quality, provenance, semantics, permissions and refresh |
| Technology/platform | fit, integration, observability, portability and lifecycle ownership |
| Evaluation and assurance | representative tests, thresholds, monitoring and independent challenge |
| Governance and accountability | risk tiers, owners, escalation, records and stakeholder protection |
| Talent and workforce | role redesign, skills, participation, incentives and transition support |
| Value realization | baselines, full cost, adoption, outcomes and benefit accountability |
| Change capacity | product management, delivery discipline, trust, feedback and learning speed |
| Ecosystem resilience | vendors, concentration, regulation, security and external dependencies |

Do not average away a critical weakness. A portfolio with strong experimentation and weak accountability is not “moderately mature”; it has a material control gap.

## Evidence sources

Seek triangulation from:

- strategy, operating plans and customer/mission measures;
- initiative inventory, costs, stage and accountable owners;
- samples of real workflows and frontline experience;
- security, privacy, legal, model-risk, audit and incident evidence;
- data architecture and access constraints;
- talent, learning, role and employee listening data;
- vendor contracts, architecture decisions and exit options;
- measured pilot results, including failed or stopped work.

Label claims based only on executive perception.

## Leadership archetypes

Use archetypes only to shape intervention, never to stereotype individuals:

- **Delegators:** see AI as a technical program; need enterprise decision ownership and workflow exposure.
- **Experimenters:** sponsor many pilots; need portfolio coherence, thresholds and stopping discipline.
- **Controllers:** focus on downside; need risk-tiered experimentation and direct evidence.
- **Promoters:** emphasize narrative; need economics, operational proof and transparent limitations.
- **Operators:** focus on efficiency; need customer, workforce and business-model horizons.
- **Strategists:** see long-term possibility; need near-term learning loops and execution ownership.

## Diagnostic output

Present:

1. enterprise context and confidence of the assessment;
2. maturity heatmap by dimension and material business unit;
3. strengths that can create advantage;
4. gaps that block value or create unacceptable exposure;
5. no-regret actions, contested choices and experiments;
6. leadership behaviors that must change;
7. evidence still required.
