# Strategy and Portfolio

## Strategic thesis

Express the enterprise AI thesis in six parts:

1. **Value:** stakeholder or business outcomes AI could materially change.
2. **Advantage:** proprietary context, data, workflow position, trust, talent or distribution that makes the change defensible.
3. **Horizon:** the chosen balance of Assist, Redesign and Reinvent.
4. **Capabilities:** data, platform, product, talent, governance and change abilities required.
5. **Boundaries:** activities the enterprise will not automate or pursue now.
6. **Learning agenda:** uncertainties to resolve and evidence needed for the next capital decision.

Avoid generic ambitions such as “be AI-first.” A strategy must reveal choices and opportunity cost.

## Portfolio categories

Maintain visibility across:

- workforce enablement and safe everyday use;
- functional productivity and quality;
- end-to-end value-stream redesign;
- customer, citizen or employee experience;
- product/service innovation and new revenue or mission capability;
- data, platform, security, evaluation and governance foundations;
- strategic options, research and ecosystem partnerships.

Foundations should be funded in proportion to real portfolio needs. Do not turn “foundation” into an indefinite program detached from use cases.

## Opportunity framing

For each material initiative require:

| Element | Executive question |
|---|---|
| Outcome | Which strategic or stakeholder result changes? |
| Baseline | What happens today, at what quality, cost, speed and risk? |
| Workflow | What end-to-end work and decision rights change? |
| Advantage | Why can this enterprise create distinctive value? |
| Readiness | Are context, data, integration, ownership and users ready? |
| Risk | Who could be harmed and which obligations or trust conditions apply? |
| Economics | What are build, run, oversight, change and failure costs? |
| Evidence | Which tests and thresholds justify the next stage? |
| Owner | Which business executive owns outcomes and consequences? |

## Portfolio scoring

Use explicit judgment across value, strategic differentiation, reach, time to evidence, readiness, change load, full economics, reversibility and risk. Show confidence and dependencies. Do not let a composite score approve an initiative.

Plot candidates by horizon and risk tier to expose a portfolio that is all quick wins, all moonshots or all infrastructure.

## Stage gates

### Discovery

Evidence: user/workflow problem, baseline, plausible AI role, authoritative context, affected stakeholders, initial risk and economics.

Decision: reject, research further or authorize a bounded pilot.

### Pilot

Evidence: representative cases, comparison baseline, quality/safety thresholds, adoption behavior, override/failure data, run-cost estimate and accountable owner.

Decision: stop, revise, extend or prepare controlled deployment.

### Scale

Evidence: sustained outcomes, operating readiness, monitoring, assurance, workforce plan, support model, contracts, data lifecycle and funded ownership.

Decision: scale by defined cohort/value stream or contain.

### Operate and renew

Evidence: value trend, incidents, drift, cost, user/workforce effects, control effectiveness and changing external conditions.

Decision: improve, retrain/reconfigure, renegotiate, restrict, replace or retire.

## Economics

Include:

- technology and consumption cost;
- integration, data/context preparation and evaluation;
- security, legal, assurance and monitoring;
- workflow redesign, training, transition and support;
- human review and exception handling;
- vendor concentration and switching/exit cost;
- realized benefit, capacity disposition and cannibalization;
- cost of failure, delay and opportunity.

Avoid claiming labor savings unless the operating plan explains how released capacity becomes value.

## Executive and board narrative

Use a concise sequence:

1. What changed externally and why it matters now.
2. What the enterprise has learned from evidence.
3. Where advantage and exposure sit.
4. Which horizon and portfolio choices management recommends.
5. What is being funded, stopped or deferred.
6. How accountability, workforce and stakeholder protections work.
7. Which measures and thresholds the board will see.
8. Which decisions or risk-appetite guidance are requested.

Separate facts, assumptions, scenarios and management judgment.

## Roadmap

Prefer decision horizons over a feature list:

- **Next 90 days:** align leaders, inventory portfolio, close urgent policy/control gaps, baseline value streams and run a few evidence-rich pilots.
- **3–12 months:** scale proven H1/H2 capabilities, establish platform/evaluation patterns, redesign priority workflows and build role-based capability.
- **12–24 months:** expand AI-native value streams, retire obsolete process, reshape roles and suppliers, and exercise H3 strategic options supported by evidence.

Adjust timing to regulatory, technical, workforce and organizational reality.
