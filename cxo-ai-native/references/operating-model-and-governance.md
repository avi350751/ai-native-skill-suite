# Operating Model and Governance

## Design choices

Make explicit choices for:

- **accountability:** business executives own outcomes; named risk/control owners retain their mandates;
- **coordination:** central, federated, hub-and-spoke or embedded arrangements based on scale, variation and risk;
- **delivery:** durable cross-functional product/value-stream teams rather than isolated proofs of concept;
- **platform:** reusable access, data/context, identity, tooling, evaluation, observability and cost controls;
- **governance:** risk-tiered requirements built into intake, delivery and operation;
- **learning:** communities, role-based practice, incident learning and reusable patterns;
- **funding:** portfolio stages, product lifecycle cost and capacity for continuous evaluation.

Avoid creating a central AI team that owns every outcome or a fully federated model that duplicates controls and platforms.

## Decision rights

Define who can:

- approve tools and vendors;
- authorize data classes and integrations;
- accept use-case and residual risk;
- approve external or consequential actions;
- set evaluation thresholds;
- deploy, pause and retire a workflow;
- investigate incidents and notify affected parties or authorities;
- commit capital and recognize benefits;
- change jobs, performance measures or workforce plans.

Use a RACI only after the decisions themselves are clear.

## Risk-tiered lifecycle

Classify from context and impact, not the model label alone.

- **Low:** reversible internal assistance using approved data; lightweight checks and user responsibility.
- **Moderate:** material workflow or customer/employee influence; documented evaluation, owner, monitoring and human review.
- **High:** legal rights, safety, employment, finance, healthcare, critical infrastructure or other consequential domains; specialist review, robust validation, strong human authority, auditability and jurisdiction-specific compliance.
- **Prohibited or paused:** uses inconsistent with law, values, rights, risk appetite or available controls.

For every tier address privacy, security, IP, bias/fairness, reliability, transparency, records, third parties, misuse, resilience and human accountability as relevant. Verify applicable legal requirements from current official sources.

## Governance embedded in delivery

The control path should include:

1. use-case intake and accountable owner;
2. data/context and affected-stakeholder assessment;
3. risk classification and required specialists;
4. evaluation plan and acceptance thresholds;
5. pre-deployment review proportionate to risk;
6. monitoring, feedback, incident and override mechanisms;
7. periodic value/control review and retirement decision.

Governance should accelerate known-safe patterns and concentrate scrutiny where impact and uncertainty are high.

## Workforce and organization

Address four levels:

- **work:** tasks, decisions and handoffs that change;
- **roles:** judgment, skills, accountability and workload that change;
- **teams:** composition, manager practices and coordination that change;
- **enterprise:** workforce planning, talent markets, learning, employee relations and culture that change.

Engage affected workers and representatives early where appropriate. Evaluate work intensification, deskilling, surveillance, accessibility, fairness, job quality and transition support—not only headcount.

Capability building should combine executive fluency, shared safe-use fundamentals, role-specific practice, specialist depth and manager reinforcement. Link training to live workflows and assessed evidence.

## Technology and vendor posture

Require clarity on:

- architecture and integration boundaries;
- data use, retention, training and residency;
- identity, authorization and action permissions;
- model/tool evaluation and change notification;
- observability, incident response and service resilience;
- intellectual property and indemnities;
- unit economics and consumption controls;
- portability, concentration and exit plan;
- subcontractors and supply-chain risk.

Do not let procurement questionnaires substitute for operational testing.

## Enterprise measures

Use a balanced view:

- strategic/business or mission outcomes;
- customer/citizen/employee outcomes;
- workflow quality, speed and capacity;
- financial value and full run cost;
- adoption in eligible workflows;
- incidents, overrides, drift and control effectiveness;
- workforce skill, trust, mobility and job-quality effects;
- learning speed, reuse and percentage of initiatives stopped for sound reasons.

Avoid a single enterprise “AI ROI” number that conceals differing horizons and risk.

## Continuous sensing loop

Establish a cadence that connects external change, operational observation, assurance, portfolio review and strategy adjustment. Owners should monitor capability shifts, regulation, vendor changes, incidents, cost curves, competitive/customer behavior and workforce evidence. Define which changes trigger re-evaluation or immediate containment.
